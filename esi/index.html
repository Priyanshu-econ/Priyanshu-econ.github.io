The raw indicators come from the World Bank (World Development Indicators and the Global Database of Inflation, both CC BY 4.0), Our World in Data (CC BY 4.0) and the U.S. Energy Information Administration (public domain). If you use the raw values, please credit those sources as well. About 3% of indicator values were missing in the sources and were filled by linear interpolation or by carrying the nearest year forward; each one is flagged in the data.

Method

The index follows the OECD and JRC handbook on composite indicators. The methodology PDF gives every choice in full.

DimensionIndicatorUnitDirectionSource
  1. Min–max normalisation, pooled over all countries and years

    Each indicator is rescaled to 0–1 using the minimum and maximum across all 1,000 country-years, so scores are comparable across countries and over time. Indicators where a lower value means more security are reversed.

    p = (x − min) / (max − min)  or  p = (max − x) / (max − min)
  2. Equal weights

    Each dimension gets a weight of 0.25 and each indicator 0.5 within its dimension, so all eight indicators weigh 12.5% overall.

  3. Arithmetic mean within each dimension

    The two indicators of a dimension can offset each other.

    D = ½ (p₁ + p₂)
  4. Geometric mean across dimensions

    A weak dimension cannot be fully offset by a strong one, so balanced energy systems score higher.

    ESI = (DAvail × DAccess × DAfford × DAccept)¼
  5. Sensitivity

    The index is recomputed under 1,000 random weightings and with arithmetic aggregation.

Scores are relative to this sample: 1 is the best value observed among these 40 countries over 2000–2024, not an absolute target. Adding countries or years changes the minimum and maximum and therefore the scores, so cite the version you use.

The paper

    
Geopolitical Risk and Energy Security: Quantile and Logit Evidence from a Composite Index

    
Priyanshu Chavda · Job market paper · Pandit Deendayal Energy University

    
Energy security has re-emerged as a central policy concern amid recurring geopolitical shocks, decarbonisation commitments and rapid technological change. This paper examines how geopolitical risk, financial development, green trade and technological innovation shape energy security across economies with different levels of security, using a multidimensional Energy Security Index built on the Availability, Accessibility, Affordability and Acceptability (4-A) framework for 40 economies over 2000–2024, Method of Moments Quantile Regression, IV-GMM and a logit model of high- and low-security regimes.
